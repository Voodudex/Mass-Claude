# Smart-money day-trading method (55-day boot camp)

## Where this came from

A 55-day YouTube boot camp that alternates technical lessons with mindset lessons. Gemini watched each video and summarized it; Claude condensed those summaries. Timestamps and quotes come from those summaries, which were **not** checked against captions or frames. Treat exact wording as approximate. The method is discretionary: it needs judgment on which highs, lows and zones "count", and the course never states a measured win rate or long-run track record.

## 1. Building blocks (definitions as taught)

**Candles (Day 2).** Only judge a candle once it has closed. Single-candle reads only. Wicks show rejection, dojis (small body, long wicks) show indecision or exhaustion, and a strong full-body close shows intent. Multi-candle patterns like head and shoulders or W bottoms are rejected.

**Trend (Day 4).** An uptrend makes higher highs and higher lows; a downtrend makes lower highs and lower lows. Otherwise the market is consolidating. A line chart helps see the higher-timeframe trend. Don't trade against it.

**Highs, lows and break of structure (Day 6).**
- A **high** is a move up then down (at least 2 candles), marked at the highest wick. A **low** is the mirror image.
- A **BOS** happens when a candle **body closes** beyond the most recent high or low. A wick through the level does **not** count; that's a sweep or fakeout.
- Only the most recent high or low matters.

**Liquidity (Days 8, 10, 12).** Resting orders (stops plus pending buy/sell orders) above prominent highs and below prominent lows. They act as magnets, because large players need those orders to fill their positions.
- **Liquidity sweep:** price pushes past a prominent high or low, takes the orders, and reacts.
- **Draw on liquidity:** the next pool price is likely heading to, and the natural target.
- Session highs and lows (Asia, London, New York) are key pools.

**Fair value gap / imbalance / liquidity void (Days 14, 16, 18).** A fast impulsive move that left no opposing orders.
- **Drawn on 3 candles:** for a bullish FVG, from the top of candle 1's wick to the bottom of candle 3's wick; the bearish one is the mirror. If the wicks of candles 1 and 3 overlap, there's no FVG.
- **Use:** a retracement or continuation zone, not a reversal signal and not a blind entry. Wait for a reaction or a lower-timeframe BOS.

**Order block (Days 20, 22, 24).** The leg that caused the sweep, just before the BOS: the down-leg before a bullish BOS, or the up-leg before a bearish BOS. Price often returns there to fill more orders. There is one order block per trend leg.
- **Beginner drawing:** box the whole leg.
- **Advanced drawing:** box from the wick to where the candle body starts.

**Equilibrium, premium and discount (Days 26, 28).** Measure the swing with a fib tool at levels 0, 0.5 and 1. Buy below 50% (discount) and sell above 50% (premium). The instructor calls the favourable side for either direction "discount", meaning "on sale". It's a zone to wait in, never an entry by itself.

**Timeframe hierarchy (Day 36).** Higher timeframes hold more power.
- A 15m BOS is often just a 1h retrace, a 1h BOS just a 4h retrace, a 4h BOS just a daily retrace, and a daily BOS just a weekly retrace.
- Don't read a lower-timeframe BOS as a reversal of the bigger trend.
- **Trade the extension** (the leg in the higher-timeframe direction), not the retracement.
- If price reaches a marked zone and doesn't react, treat the zone as if it never existed.

## 2. Daily bias: top-down process (Days 34-36, 43, 50)

1. **Weekly (and monthly):** macro direction and the big draws on liquidity.
2. **Daily:** trend structure, which is the **daily bias**. The course calls this the most important skill.
3. **4H / 1H:** find building blocks in line with the bias (order blocks, FVGs, equilibrium) and the nearest draws on liquidity.
4. **15m / 5m / 1m:** execution only. Wait for a sweep plus a lower-timeframe BOS inside a higher-timeframe zone.
5. **Before the session,** write the bias, the zones and the targets. Accept being wrong when structure flips: adapt, don't force it.
6. **Weekend:** do the weekly analysis and note the week's news calendar.

## 3. Entry models (Day 30, ranked by risk)

1. **Sweep + BOS:** most risky. The instructor later says this alone kept getting him stopped out (Day 41).
2. **Sweep + BOS + order-block retest.**
3. **Sweep + BOS + FVG fill.**
4. **Sweep + BOS + order block or FVG + price in discount/premium at equilibrium:** the safest, recommended for beginners.

Every model also needs: **alignment with the daily bias** and higher timeframes, the confirming candle **closed** (no wicks, no anticipating), and a trending rather than choppy market (Day 44). Pick one model, write it in the plan, and don't change strategy, only refine it.

## 4. Stops, targets and management (Days 16, 22, 37, 38, 53)

- **Stop** at the invalidation point: just beyond the liquidity sweep (above the swept high for shorts, below the swept low for longs), plus a small spread buffer (about 0.5 points on indices). The stop must match the timeframe traded: a 4H or daily setup needs its stop beyond the whole order block.
- **Targets:** building blocks and draws on liquidity on the execution timeframe or one higher, such as prior highs/lows, session highs/lows, opposing FVGs and order blocks, or the 50% of an imbalance.
- **Take-profit ladder:** 3-4 TPs, with **TP1 at least 1:1**. Move the stop to break-even after TP1. Close a trade that is taking too long.

## 5. Risk and position size (Days 13, 27, 29, 39)

- **Risk** 1% per trade as standard; 1-3% per **day** at most, across all trades.
- **Trades per day:** usually 1, at most 2 when de-risked so both together equal one normal trade. Walk away after the trade, win or lose.
- **Risk tiers:** de-risk (half size) on news weeks, bank holidays and uncertain conditions; normal; and "confident" double size, which beginners must **never** use. Never go all in.
- **Lot size** = (balance × risk %) ÷ (stop distance in pips or points × value per pip or point for 1 lot). Check the broker's **contract size** under the symbol details, because it differs between brokers and prop firms.
- **Account stages:** demo first. Then a tiny live account (for example $100 at 1% risk) so losses carry no emotion. Don't change habits or raise leverage when moving from demo to live.

## 6. Sessions, markets and news (Days 19, 29, 34, 46, 47, 50)

- **Markets:** master **one** pair or market first. The course used S&P 500, GBP/USD, GBP/JPY and gold, and avoided AUD, CAD, CHF and NZD pairs.
- **Sessions:** New York or London, never Asia. For S&P, trade the New York open, not London. Take trades in the first ~1.5 hours of the chosen session.
- **News:** check Forex Factory every morning.
  - **Never trade CPI, PPI, FOMC, NFP, or Fed-chair speeches.** Skip bank holidays.
  - **Other red-folder news:** wait 15-30 minutes after the release. Skip the day if it caused wild wicks, and de-risk if you do trade.
  - **Currency-pair math:** good news for the base (left) currency pushes the pair up; good news for the quote (right) currency pushes it down. Price action overrides news bias.
  - **News before the open:** pre-market releases can exhaust the move before the open, which leaves chop. Treat that as a no-trade day and do live testing instead.

## 7. Trading plan template (Day 29)

Write it once, keep it in front of you, and follow it robotically:
- **Market:** the one pair or market.
- **Session and window:** which session, and the first ~1.5 hours of it.
- **News policy:** which events to skip, and how to de-risk.
- **Risk:** % per trade, tier rules, max trades and max loss per day.
- **Bias process:** the weekly, daily, 4H, 1H steps.
- **Entry model:** one of the 4 above.
- **Stop and target rules:** invalidation, TP1 at least 1:1, the TP ladder, break-even.
- **Post-trade:** journal, then stop for the day.

## 8. Journal fields (Day 46)

For each trade:
- pair, session, date
- daily, 4H, 1H and 15m bias, and the confluences used
- entry, stop, TPs, risk %
- whether the plan was followed (yes/no)
- emotions before, during and after, and why
- result in R, and what to improve

Review the journal at the end of the day or month, not after every trade. Judge results by month and year, not by single trades.

## 9. Practice drills from the course

- **Spotting:** 10 dojis and wick rejections; 10 real BOS and 10 fakeouts; 15 liquidity sweeps (5 on each of 3 timeframes); 10 valid and some invalid FVGs; 5 order blocks on each of 3 timeframes; 10 examples each of the entry models.
- **Backtesting:** scroll back through real charts across timeframes instead of using bar replay, which locks you to one timeframe and removes live emotion (Day 42). Review the past week daily and find 1-2 valid trades.
- **Live testing:** on news days, write the bias before the open, mark the mock trades, and review the result after.

## 10. Mindset rules (the other half of the course)

- **Discipline over motivation:** motivation fades in about a week, and habits take roughly 21-60 days.
- **Losses:** expect to lose in years 1-2, which is tuition. One trade, then done; no revenge trades.
- **Wins:** treat them like losses, emotionlessly. Don't post P&L. Overconfidence after a win leads to overtrading.
- **Not trading:** look for reasons *not* to trade. Zero-trade days are good days.
- **Your mistakes:** every mistake is your fault. Write down your problems and solve the root cause.
- **Your own process:** one strategy, no signal groups, and form your own bias. Don't trade alongside friends.
- **Burnout:** rest, sleep, and take breaks. The course expects about a year or more of screen time after the boot camp before profitability.

## How to use this skill

- **Analyzing a chart or data:** go top-down (section 2). State the bias, the zones, the draws on liquidity and which entry model would apply. Name any missing confluence and any news or session conflict. Say "no trade" when the checklist fails.
- **When asked to automate it:** point out which parts are discretionary ("prominent" highs, which leg is the order block, choppy vs trending) and need an explicit rule before they can be coded or backtested.
- **Never place real orders** without the user's explicit go-ahead for that specific trade. Default to demo or paper trading, as the course itself does.
- **Profitability:** the course shows hand-picked historical examples and recaps, not a verified track record. Rules similar in spirit (a VWAP / EMA / opening-range options strategy) showed no edge in an honest 2-year test (see the `options-signals` skill). Say so if asked whether this method "works".
